# Strength

The Strength tab shows the monthly calendar, **Log Session**, and **Saved Workouts**. Both entry points ask for **Planned session** or **Current / completed session**. A plan takes a future start time. A completed entry takes an end time at or before now. Strength month markers are outlined for plans and filled for completed logs, with date placement based on start and end respectively. Past plans retain their planned status. Home uses its existing Strength category colour.

## Exercise table

`src/screens/strength/WorkoutBuilderScreen.tsx` keeps the shared header, fixed Cancel / Save or Unsave / Plan or Log footer, and inline duration and focus estimates. `src/components/strength/ExerciseTable.tsx` puts the catalog search and exercise editing on the same screen. The thin tertiary search sits above a page-level results overlay, with exercise name, first two target muscles, equipment and a gold favourite star. Favourites still use `caps.strength.exercise-favourites.v1` in Expo's SQLite key-value store. The catalog seed remains text-only. The previous picker, exercise editor and exercise images were removed.

Tables keep the exercise name, Sets, Rep Ranges, Targeted RPE, Targeted RIR and Notes in that order. The four numeric or range cells scroll directly. A tap on Rep Ranges opens its anchored list. RPE accepts 1–10 and RIR 0–10+, with an unset option for either. Rest in seconds sits beneath the effort fields. A completed exercise expands inline to show numbered sets and editable actual reps, RPE and RIR. Reps are required for every set. RPE and RIR are optional. Actual fields start empty even when opened from a template. A plan has exercise-level inputs only.

Save/Unsave acts on the reusable exercise targets independently from the session action. The session action retains source-template provenance and commits a snapshot without rewriting the template. Previous template snapshots remain readable. For a legacy template without explicit ranges, its first set supplies a supported rep range and the exercise-level target defaults. A legacy one-rep entry maps to 1–3 because it may have been submaximal. Confirmed saves use the new exercise-level target format.

## Adaptations and future recovery

`src/strength/emphasis.ts` writes `strength-emphasis-v2`. Plans expand the targeted set count in memory and estimate reps from the range midpoint, with 20+ estimated at 20. Completed estimates use the entered reps and recorded set effort. Targeted rest may inform elapsed-duration estimates and stays out of adaptation scoring for newly completed sets. Explicit RIR takes precedence over the coarse RPE fallback. A primary still requires at least 60 points and a 15-point lead. Previously saved v1 results are read as stored.

- **Hypertrophy** increases for challenging sets at 0–2 RIR and falls at larger reserves. A trained-lifter trial reported similar quadriceps growth at 1–2 RIR and at failure. A newer elbow-flexor trial also found similar size and strength gains at the tested ~2 RIR prescription and failure. The app's thresholds and weights are heuristics. [Refalo and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/) · [Vieira and colleagues](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-0413/5/3/61)
- **Strength** prefers prior same-lift load evidence and uses low reps plus a low reserve as a fallback. **Power** uses explosive intent and repetition count. A velocity-loss trial found that lower velocity loss favoured jump gains with similar squat strength gains. RIR provides no bar-speed measurement. [Cormie and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20139780/) · [Pareja-Blanco and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27038416/)
- **Muscular endurance** uses high reps, rest and reported effort as available. A high-rep intervention favoured the tested bench-press endurance outcome. RIR remains a self-estimate, with accuracy varying by load and exercise. [Schoenfeld and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25853914/) · [Hughes and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33337690/)
- **Recovery follow-up** should use targeted sets, rep range, RPE and RIR for planned forecasts. Use entered sets and actual effort for logged recovery. Carry missing effort as unknown. A bench-press crossover found greater acute fatigue closer to failure, with the size and duration of the effect specific to the tested workload. See [[Recovery and Progress]]. [Refalo and colleagues](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00554-y)

## Persistence and evidence

`src/data/database.ts` adds the v12 columns transactionally. `strength_sessions.timeline_entry_id` still points to one unique `timeline_entries` row, whose status owns the planned/completed distinction. The Strength header stores start and estimated end for plans, estimated start and selected end for completed sessions. The planned `scheduled_local_date` lets the monthly query anchor overnight plans to their start day. Legacy records retain their end date and existing timeline status. Source-template IDs remain provenance.

Child exercise snapshots retain their stable catalog reference and have nullable `target_json` for set count, range, target RPE/RIR, rest and any inherited target load. New target-based templates and planned sessions have no set child rows. Completed sessions have an ordered set snapshot under each exercise, with actual reps and nullable RPE/RIR. Integer RIR 10 stores the lower bound 10+. Legacy set snapshots and their cascade relationships remain. Template archive leaves session snapshots intact. The session, timeline, exercises and estimate commit together. See [[Data and Persistence]].

`npm run verify:strength` covers the v11 upgrade and rollback, v1 reads, overnight schedule, end/start validation, planned and completed snapshots, target-only template save, completed scoring independence and retry. See [[2026-11-26 Planned and Completed Strength Handoff]] for integrated and device verification.
